01 / THE BIG IDEAYOUNG CITY · ANCIENT LAND

Start with a contradiction

The city is young.
The story isn't.

Navoi was founded in 1958. But the region around it carries traces of people, trade and belief from long before the city had a name.

Think of Navoi as a modern base camp for exploring a much older world: prehistoric engravings in the hills, a royal Silk Road stop in the steppe, sacred springs and a vast lake under an enormous sky.

02 / TWO TIMELINESTHE LAND & THE CITY

One place, different clocks

Time leaves
layers.

The oldest landmarks are not in the city centre. They sit in the wider region — a reminder that modern borders rarely match the age of a place.

PREHISTORY → MIDDLE AGES

Stories cut into stone

At Sarmishsay, figures of animals and people were carved into the rock over many centuries.

THE OLDEST LAYER
11TH–12TH CENTURIES

A road through the steppe

Rabat-i Malik served as a fortified residence and later a caravan stop on a Silk Road route.

TRADE & WATER
1958

A new city takes shape

Navoi was founded alongside the development of the mining and metallurgical industry in the Kyzylkum region.

THE MODERN CHAPTER
1967 → 1969

Gold changes the map

Mining began at Muruntau in 1967; the first gold bar from the operation was produced in 1969.

INDUSTRY & INNOVATION

The ancient dates refer to sites in the wider Navoi region — not to the founding of the city itself.

05 / PEOPLE & NAMESLEGACY ISN'T ONLY ABOUT STONES

Names that travel

From a poet's name
to new voices.

The city honours a literary giant from another century — while the region continues to produce athletes, musicians and artists of its own.

A / THE NAMESAKE
ع
1441—1501

Alisher Navoi

Poet, thinker and statesman. Born in Herat, he became a defining figure of Uzbek literature. The city and region carry his name — he did not live in the modern city, founded centuries later.

06 / WHY IT STAYS WITH YOUTHE TAKEAWAY

So, why Navoi?

Because it
doesn't fit
one category.

01

For history that feels real

Rock engravings and caravanserai ruins make the region's long past tangible, not abstract.

02

For the landscape

Desert, hills, spring water and Aydarkul's open horizon offer a different side of Uzbekistan.

03

For a modern story

The city shows how industry and infrastructure can transform a region — and how life continues around that work.

04

For unexpected contrasts

In a single trip, you can move from a working city to ancient art, then end the day under a huge desert sky.

✳
Small travel note

Spring and autumn are generally more comfortable for outdoor exploring than the hottest part of summer. Carry water, sun protection and check local conditions before heading into remote areas.
